# Treatment evidence, resistance, immune biology and organoid validation

**Search cut-off 27 November 2026.** This is the clinical and organoid component of the initial review of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). The numbers below come from source reports. The judgements about transferability and priority are this programme's interpretation. No patient-level analysis or treatment assignment was performed.

## Search and selection

The [query log](clinical-queries.tsv) and [screening ledger](clinical-hits.tsv) preserve all 146 returned positions from 24 discovery searches, including duplicate locations of the same article. The unit counted there is a search hit. Do not add these counts to those from the other review components without deduplication. Search terms in the log are verbatim, including paper and trial names used for targeted retrieval. Indexed web search covered PubMed, PubMed Central, journal sites, registries, agency records, bioRxiv and medRxiv. Windows include published and posted records through the cut-off. The 12 supplementary records have individual design or extraction caveats in the last column of the ledger. Only selected searches used explicit date operators. Year-labelled search terms alone do not enforce date limits. Some supporting links were followed separately to verify results, dates and corrections.

Include human comparative trials and registry records for disease setting and endpoints, relevant PDAC organoid and mechanistic primary studies, systematic evidence syntheses and recent preprints. Select at least one negative comparative result where available. Record participant, model and biopsy denominators separately. Exclude unrelated or mixed-cancer efficacy estimates lacking separable PDAC data, promotional notices and duplicate reports as independent observations. Use conference results for horizon scanning only. Titles/abstracts and selected full text were assessed by one reviewer. This bounded search prioritised the named questions and negative controls. It does not estimate a pooled effect, the prevalence of mechanisms or the absence of other work.

The organoid sensitivity search was also executed natively against PubMed and Europe PMC (Europe PubMed Central) after initial web endpoint failures. On 27 November 2026 PubMed returned 35 identifiers and Europe PMC returned 51. The union contained 52, including four posted preprints. The narrower recent Europe PMC window returned four of the same 51. The [native screening ledger](clinical-native-screen.tsv) records every identifier, index membership, indexed date, title, publication type, decision and rationale. The 52 source records comprise 24 empirical inclusions, one stand-alone preprint, 11 secondary or contextual entries, five linked versions or corrections, and 11 exclusions. This covers every result of these specified queries. PubMed used ESearch with database `pubmed`, JSON output and `retmax=1000`. Europe PMC used its REST search endpoint with `resultType=core`, `format=json` and `pageSize=300`. Each index returned fewer items than its page limit. The exact PubMed string was:

("pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma" OR "pancreatic cancer") AND (TITLE:organoid OR TITLE:organoids) AND (TITLE:pharmacotyping OR TITLE:prospective OR TITLE:chemotherapy OR TITLE:prediction) AND FIRST_PDATE:[2015-01-01 TO 2026-11-27]

The recent Europe PMC sensitivity run replaced only the lower date bound with 2026-09-01. These organoid title strings are supplemental sensitivity searches. They omit papers using different terminology and cannot serve as the entire clinical review. The scoped assessment should be updated with two independent screeners and domain-specific risk-of-bias assessment before external publication. No review preregistration took place in this pass.

## Organoids, resistance and novelty

A patient-derived organoid (PDO) is a laboratory model. Correlating its short-term response with a subsequent clinical observation involves a different evidence chain from comparing clinical outcomes after randomised treatment assignment.

Authors described individual cases, longitudinal profiles and transcriptomic response signatures. | Generic pharmacotyping and using expression to project response already have precedence. | Driehuis et al., 9 December 2019 online | Thirty lines derived from pancreatic and distal bile duct tumours. ## Decision and falsification

**Candidate replication question.** Does an *existing* locked PDO-based predictor of a prespecified single agent's measured ex vivo response improve over a training-cohort mean in a donor-disjoint PDAC laboratory cohort? Match agent identity, concentration units/range, duration, response orientation and mode of administration. Example discovery and verification sources are Tiriac and Driehuis. Component assays on oxaliplatin or gemcitabine cannot establish effectiveness of a multidrug regimen. Any subsequent claim of patient benefit would need blinded prospective testing and a trial with patient-relevant endpoints. The cross-laboratory question may have value as an assay-reliability result even if it fails.

**Feasibility conditions.** Confirm access to a frozen predictor with reproducible preprocessing, both cohorts' paired features and measurements, shared assay domain, separable ductal histology, donor-disjoint identifiers and a useful precision at the anticipated sample size. If response is ranked only within each cohort, compare ranks or calibrate on training data only and explicitly change the estimand. Without paired response labels, a cross-cohort correlation of *predictions* cannot count as validation. If there are no paired donors across laboratories, a between-donor holdout can test prediction transportability, but cannot identify a pure laboratory effect. Record covariate overlap and apply prespecified missingness exclusions before viewing test outcomes. No dataset pair has been joined, checksummed or declared adequate here.

**Decision.** Continue metadata, prior-art and shared-measure verification. The broad propositions that organoids differ in drug sensitivity, that treatment changes cell state, that the microenvironment can confer resistance, and that an RNA therapeutic can engage a biological target all have published precedents. Originality would depend on a narrower unresolved claim. Report a design failure if the available data cannot distinguish it from prior work.
